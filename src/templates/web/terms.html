Not an emergency service

If this is an emergency, do not use {{ app_name }}. Go to your nearest emergency department, or call 10177 for an ambulance or 112 from a mobile phone. {{ app_name }} manages a queue. It does not triage you, it does not assess how urgent your condition is, and no one is watching it for people who need help now. Joining a queue is not a request for emergency care and will not be treated as one.

{{ app_name }} also does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. Nothing in the application is a substitute for the judgement of a healthcare professional. If your condition changes while you are waiting, tell a staff member at the clinic.

Project status

Read this first. {{ app_name }} is a final-year BSc IT capstone project at Richfield Graduate Institute of Technology, and is not yet operating in a live clinic. These terms describe how the service is intended to be provided. They are not legal advice. They must be reviewed by a suitably qualified person, and completed with the deployment's own supplier details, before any real patient uses the service.

Who these terms are between

These terms are an agreement between you and the operator of this {{ app_name }} deployment. They cover your use of the application, the USSD and WhatsApp channels, the self-service kiosk and this website.

They are not an agreement about your healthcare. Your care is provided by the clinic, on the clinic's terms and under its professional and legal duties. {{ app_name }} carries your place in its queue; it does not treat you, and it does not decide who the clinic sees or in what order beyond running the order the clinic configures.

By using the service you agree to these terms. If you do not agree, do not use it.

What the service does

  • Helps you find clinics near you, with their sector, opening status and current queue length.
  • Lets you take a place in a clinic's queue: from the app, over USSD, on WhatsApp, on the web, at the kiosk, or at reception.
  • Shows you your position and an estimated wait, and notifies you as your turn approaches.
  • Shows your ticket number on the clinic's waiting-room screen, and your name only where you have consented and the clinic has enabled it.
  • Gives the clinic a dashboard to run the queue, and reports on how it performed.

A place in a queue is not an appointment, and it is not a promise that you will be seen. A clinic may close early, run out of capacity, or reprioritise for clinical reasons.

Queue positions and estimates

Waiting times shown in the application are estimates, calculated from how quickly the clinic is currently working. They will change, sometimes sharply. Clinical need comes first: staff can and will move a patient ahead of you, and each such override is recorded with a reason.

Do not rely on an estimate for anything that would harm you if it were wrong. Give yourself margin, and follow whatever the clinic tells you in person over what the application shows.

Your account

  • Give accurate details, and keep your mobile number and email address current; that is how we reach you when it is your turn.
  • You are responsible for what happens under your account, and for keeping your password, one-time codes and two-factor device secure.
  • Do not share your account or let someone else use it. Tell the operator promptly if you think it has been used without your permission.
  • If you register on a shared or borrowed phone, sign out when you are done.

Booking for someone else

You may join a queue on behalf of a dependant: a child, or an adult who has asked you to. When you do, you confirm that you are entitled to act for that person and to give the consents the service records. For a child, that means you are the parent, guardian or other competent person. You can revoke a proxy arrangement at any time, and so can the person you booked for.

Acceptable use

Do not:

  • Take places in queues you do not intend to use, or take multiple places for the same visit.
  • Impersonate another person, or join a queue for someone who has not asked you to.
  • Try to reach records, clinics or accounts you have not been granted access to, or probe, scan or test the service's security without written permission.
  • Interfere with the service or its infrastructure, including automated scraping, flooding the messaging channels, or anything that degrades it for others.
  • Use the service to break the law, to harass anyone, or to send unsolicited messages.

Security researchers are welcome: report a vulnerability to the operator rather than exploiting it, and give us a reasonable chance to fix it before disclosing it.

Messages and charges

The service is free for patients to use. Your mobile network's own charges still apply: USSD session fees, data, and any charges for messages you send. We do not control those and we cannot refund them.

Messages depend on networks and third-party providers, so delivery cannot be guaranteed. Do not treat the absence of a message as confirmation that it is not yet your turn. You can change which messages you receive, or turn them off, in your notification preferences; essential service messages about your current ticket may still be sent.
