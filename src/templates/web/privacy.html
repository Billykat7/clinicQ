Project status

Read this first. {{ app_name }} is a final-year BSc IT capstone project at Richfield Graduate Institute of Technology. It is not yet operating in a live clinic, and the screens on this site carry generated, illustrative data: no real patient information. This notice describes how the system is designed to handle personal information, so that it is accurate on the day the first clinic goes live. It is not legal advice: it must be reviewed by a suitably qualified person, and completed with the deployment's own responsible-party and Information Officer details, before any real patient uses the service.

Who processes your information

POPIA distinguishes the responsible party (who decides why and how personal information is processed) from the operator, who processes it on the responsible party's behalf. {{ app_name }} is both, in different places:

  • The clinic is the responsible party for everything about your visit: that you joined its queue, the room you were sent to, the ticket you were given, and anything a staff member records about it. {{ app_name }} processes that as the clinic's operator, under a written agreement that binds us to the clinic's instructions and to POPIA's security requirements (sections 20 and 21).
  • {{ app_name }} is the responsible party for the account itself: the details you give when you register, your channel and notification preferences, your sign-in sessions, and the technical logs that keep the platform secure and available.

If you are not sure which applies to a question you have, ask us and we will route it, or ask the clinic directly.

What we process

Only what a queue actually needs. The design rule throughout is minimisation: if a field is not required to get you seen, it is either optional or absent.

  • Who you are. Your name and the mobile number or email address you register with. A date of birth only where a clinic needs it to distinguish two people with the same name.
  • How you reached us. The channel you used and its identifier: your MSISDN for USSD and SMS, your WhatsApp number, a push token for the app.
  • Your place in the queue. Your ticket number, the clinic, the room or service, the times you joined, were called and were seen, and the outcome (seen, transferred, no-show).
  • Why you are here, if you choose to say. A free-text reason or a visit note recorded by staff. This is optional, it is never shown on the waiting-room screen, and it is deleted automatically on a short retention window.
  • Where you are, if you allow it. Your device's approximate location, used at the moment you search for a nearby clinic. We do not track your location in the background, and we do not keep a location history.
  • Consent records. What you agreed to, when, through which channel, and when you withdrew it, including consent given by a parent or guardian on a dependant's behalf.
  • Technical records. Your IP address, device and browser details, sign-in times, and application logs. These keep the service secure and let us investigate faults and abuse.
  • Staff account information. For clinic staff and administrators: name, work email address, role and permission assignments, and an audit record of the administrative changes they make.
Number only, by default. Every new clinic starts with a waiting-room screen that shows ticket numbers and nothing else. Showing a first name and last initial, or a full name, is a deliberate change a clinic must make, and it depends on consent recorded per patient. The reason you are visiting is never displayed, in any mode.

Health information

Information about your health is special personal information under section 26 of POPIA, and processing it is prohibited unless one of the grounds in section 27 or section 32 applies. Two things follow from that, and both shape the product:

  • We keep as little of it as possible. A ticket number in a queue is not in itself health information. A reason for the visit, a triage priority or a visit note is. So those fields are optional, restricted to the staff who need them, excluded from the public display, and purged automatically.
  • Where we do process it, we rely on your consent (section 27(1)(a)) and, for the clinic, on section 32, which permits processing by or on behalf of a healthcare institution where it is necessary for your proper treatment and care. Everyone with access is under a duty of confidentiality.

You can withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawing consent for your name to appear on the waiting-room screen takes effect immediately and does not affect your place in the queue.

Children and dependants

Section 34 of POPIA prohibits processing the personal information of a child except on one of the grounds in section 35, including the prior consent of a competent person. {{ app_name }} supports booking on someone else's behalf (a parent for a child, an adult child for an elderly parent) and treats that as a consent event in its own right: who gave it, for whom, when, and through which channel. A proxy booking can be revoked, and the dependant's information is visible only to the person who holds that consent and to the treating clinic.

Who else sees it

  • The clinic you joined. Its staff see what they need to run the queue and treat you, limited by role.
  • Communication providers. An SMS aggregator, a WhatsApp business provider, a push-notification service and an email provider deliver the messages you have asked for. They receive your number or address and the message, and nothing more.
  • Hosting and infrastructure providers. Who store and run the service on our behalf.
  • Health departments and district managers. Only anonymised, aggregated statistics, with small counts suppressed so an individual cannot be identified from them.
  • Where the law requires it. In response to a lawful demand, or to establish, exercise or defend a legal claim.

Every provider above is an operator under POPIA, engaged under a written contract that requires them to process the information only on our instructions and to maintain the security measures section 21 requires.

Sending it outside South Africa

Section 72 restricts transferring personal information out of the Republic. Our preference is to host and process in South Africa. Where a provider (a messaging or cloud service) necessarily processes information abroad, we rely on one of the section 72 grounds: a law or binding agreement in the destination country that provides an adequate, comparable level of protection, or your consent, or that the transfer is necessary to perform the service you asked for. We will tell you which, on request.

How we protect it

Section 19 requires appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures. Ours are built into the platform rather than bolted on:

  • Encryption in transit, and encryption at rest for the fields that identify you.
  • Role-based access control with scope tiers: staff see their own clinic's records and no other clinic's. A request for another clinic's data is answered as if the record does not exist.
  • A hash-chained, append-only audit log, so a retrospective edit is detectable rather than merely discouraged.
  • Two-factor authentication for staff accounts, rate limiting on sign-in and on the messaging channels, and regular dependency and vulnerability scanning.
  • Independent penetration testing before go-live, with the findings remediated and re-tested.

If a security compromise affects your personal information, section 22 requires us to notify the Information Regulator and you, as soon as reasonably possible after discovering it. We will tell you what happened, what it means for you and what to do.

Your rights

Chapter 3 of POPIA gives you the right to:

  • Be told that we hold information about you, and what it is (sections 23 and 24).
  • Ask for a copy of it, in a usable form. We export what we hold about you.
  • Have it corrected if it is inaccurate, irrelevant, excessive, out of date, incomplete, misleading, or obtained unlawfully.
  • Have it deleted or destroyed where we no longer have a lawful reason to keep it. Some records (the audit trail, and clinical records the clinic must keep by law) cannot be erased on request; we will say so and explain why.
  • Object to processing we base on legitimate interests, on reasonable grounds (section 11(3)).
  • Withdraw consent at any time, without affecting what was lawfully done before you withdrew it.
  • Not be subject to a decision with legal consequences based solely on automated processing (section 71).
  • Complain to the Information Regulator, and to institute civil proceedings (sections 74 and 99).

To exercise any of these, contact the Information Officer. A request for access or correction may be made on the form prescribed under the POPIA regulations. We do not charge for a first access request, we answer within the period the Act allows, and we verify your identity first, so that nobody else can use these rights to reach your records.

Direct marketing

Section 69 restricts unsolicited electronic direct marketing. We do not send it. The messages you receive from {{ app_name }} are the operational ones you asked for: your place in the queue, your call-forward, your appointment reminder, your chronic-medication collection nudge. Every one of them can be turned off in your notification preferences or by replying with the opt-out keyword, and quiet hours are respected.

Automated decisions

Wait-time estimates and queue order are calculated automatically, and a priority override is always a decision a named staff member makes and records with a reason. No decision with legal consequences for you is made solely by automated processing (section 71), and you can ask a staff member to review any queue decision that affects you.

Cookies and local storage

We use the minimum a secure application needs: a session cookie and a cross-site request forgery token so that signing in works and cannot be hijacked, and your light/dark theme choice stored in your own browser. There are no advertising cookies and no third-party tracking on this site. Clearing your browser's site data removes all of it.
